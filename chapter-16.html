Chapter 16: Allocating Support-Department Costs

Parts I and II · Five short pages. Read What to do, or press Show me how for a guided walk-through.

    The setup: two support and two operating departments

    Support departments serve the operating departments and each other. All three methods end with every support cost in the operating departments. They differ only in how they treat the services the two support departments give each other:

    • Direct ignores them.
    • Step-down recognizes them in one direction only.
    • Reciprocal recognizes them in both directions.
    Which arrows does each method use?

    Direct method

    The direct method allocates each support department's cost only to the operating departments.

    • Services the support departments give each other are ignored.
    • So each support department's percentages are re-based over the operating departments only.

    Step-down method

    The step-down method allocates the support departments one at a time, in order.

    • Go first: the support department that gives the larger share of its service to the other support department.
    • It is allocated to everyone, including the other support department.
    • Once a department is allocated, nothing is allocated back to it.

    Reciprocal method

    The reciprocal method fully recognizes the services the support departments give each other. Three steps:

    1. Write one equation per support department: complete cost = own cost + its share of the other's complete cost.
    2. Solve the equations for the complete reciprocated costs.
    3. Allocate those amounts using all the percentages.

    Compare the three methods

    Every method allocates the same total support cost. Only the split between the operating departments changes.

    • Differences grow when the support departments serve each other more, and when the operating departments use the two support departments differently.
    • Reciprocal is conceptually the most precise, because it counts all the mutual services.
    • Direct and step-down are simpler to compute and understand. Direct is widely used.
