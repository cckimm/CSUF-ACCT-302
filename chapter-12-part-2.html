    Product mix: one scarce resource

    When one resource limits production:

    • Don't rank products by contribution margin per unit or by CM ratio.
    • Rank them by contribution margin per unit of the scarce resource (here, per machine-hour).
    • Make the top product first.

    Bottleneck: the slowest step sets the pace

    A bottleneck is the step with the least capacity.

    • The whole line finishes only as many units as the bottleneck can handle.
    • Extra capacity anywhere else just piles up work in process.

    Four steps to manage a bottleneck

    1. Recognize that the bottleneck determines the contribution margin of the whole system.
    2. Identify the bottleneck operation.
    3. Keep the bottleneck busy, and subordinate every non-bottleneck operation to it.
    4. Increase the bottleneck's efficiency and capacity: eliminate idle time, shift work to other machines or outside suppliers, cut setup and processing time, and improve quality.

    Drop a product line?

    A segment that shows a loss isn't automatically one to drop. If you drop it:

    • you lose its contribution margin, and
    • you save only the fixed costs that truly go away.

    Drop it only if the fixed costs saved are bigger than the contribution margin lost.

    Replace the machine?

    The old machine's book value is a sunk cost. Compare only future cash over the remaining life:

    • operating costs,
    • the price of the new machine, and
    • what the old machine sells for today.
