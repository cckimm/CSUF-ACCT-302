Chapter 9 Part I: Absorption vs. Variable Costing

Six short pages. Read, then try it.

    The company

    Both methods agree on everything except fixed manufacturing overhead, or FMOH. Absorption costing puts it inside every unit made. Variable costing expenses it right away. Tap the method that expenses all of the $120,000 right now.

    The warehouse

    Press Play. Crates leave the factory and ship to customers. Under absorption costing, fixed overhead rides inside each crate and is expensed only when the crate is sold. Under variable costing, all of it goes straight to expense.

    Each square = 500 units Orange band or coin = the fixed overhead on those 500 units

    Where the fixed overhead goes

    Variable costing expenses all of the fixed overhead right away. Absorption costing expenses only the part that is not left sitting in inventory.

    Net income

    The two net incomes differ by exactly the change in inventory units times the $12 fixed overhead rate.

    The rule

    Match your sliders to the rule. Inventory up means absorption costing net income is higher. No change means they are equal. Inventory down means absorption costing net income is lower.

    ▲Inventory increases
    production > sales    		AC net income > VC net income
    AC parks some FMOH in inventory, so it expenses less.
    ▬No change
    production = sales    		AC net income = VC net income
    No FMOH parked or released.
    ▼Inventory decreases
    production < sales    		AC net income < VC net income
    AC releases old FMOH from inventory, so it expenses more.
